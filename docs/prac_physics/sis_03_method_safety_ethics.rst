================================================
6–8. Method, Safety and Ethics
================================================

6. Materials and method
------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Key ideas
    :class: note

    * **Materials:** marble, ramp (e.g. a plastic gutter or board), books, ruler, measuring tape, masking tape, pencil.
    * A good **method** is written as **numbered steps** in order, so that someone else could repeat it exactly.
    * It states **what is measured** and **how** (e.g. measure from the end of the ramp to where the marble stops).
    * It includes **repeat trials** (e.g. three per height) and calculating an **average**.
    * It tests **several values of the IV** (e.g. 5, 10, 15 and 20 cm).

.. admonition:: Example method
    :class: hint

    #. Place books under one end of the ramp and measure the ramp height with a ruler (start at 5 cm).
    #. Mark the release point near the top of the ramp with masking tape.
    #. Hold the marble at the release point and let go without pushing it.
    #. When the marble stops, measure the distance from the end of the ramp to the marble.
    #. Record the distance in the results table.
    #. Repeat steps 3–5 until three trials are completed at this height.
    #. Calculate the average distance for this height.
    #. Raise the ramp by 5 cm and repeat steps 3–7 for heights of 10, 15 and 20 cm.

.. admonition:: Watch out
    :class: warning

    Use **masking tape** to mark the release point and the end of the ramp, so every trial starts and is measured from exactly the same place.

7. Safety: risk assessment
------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Key ideas
    :class: note

    * A **hazard** is something that could cause harm (equipment, chemicals, situations).
    * The **risk** is the harm that the hazard could cause and how likely it is.
    * A **control measure** is the action or protective equipment used to reduce or remove the risk.

.. list-table::
   :widths: 25 35 40
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Hazard
     - Risk (possible harm)
     - Control measure
   * - Marble rolling across the floor
     - Someone could step on it, slip and fall.
     - Put a box or barrier at the end of the test area to stop the marble; pick it up straight after each trial.
   * - Books holding up the ramp
     - The stack could topple onto feet or hands.
     - Build a stable stack on the floor; don't stack too high.
   * - Glass marble
     - Could chip or break if dropped, leaving sharp pieces.
     - Release it gently from the ramp, never drop it; tell the teacher about any breakage.

.. admonition:: Watch out
    :class: warning

    A control measure must be a specific **action**. "Be careful" is not enough.

8. Ethical considerations: honest results
------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Key ideas
    :class: note

    * Scientists must record and report **all** results **honestly**, including unexpected ones.
    * An **outlier** (anomaly) is a result that is very different from the others in the same set.
    * Never change, delete or ignore a result just because it doesn't match what you expected. That is **bias** and is dishonest.
    * Instead: **record it**, check for a possible error (e.g. the marble hit a table leg), **repeat the trial**, and **explain** in your report if you leave it out of the average.
    * Never make up data, and never copy another group's results without saying so.

.. admonition:: Example scenario
    :class: hint

    A student gets 109 cm at the 20 cm height. They want to write down 120 cm instead, because that is the true value.
    They **should not change it**. Changing data to match the expected answer is dishonest and hides real errors.
    They should record 109 cm, look for a reason and repeat the trial.
