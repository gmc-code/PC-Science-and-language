======================================
2B How Biologists Classify Organisms
======================================

The Criteria for Life
==================================

* **Living** things carry out seven life processes.
* Life processes are remembered as **MRS GREN**.
* **Movement** means moving parts of the body.
* **Respiration** releases energy from food.
* **Sensitivity** detects and responds to changes.
* **Growth** increases the size of organisms.
* **Reproduction** creates offspring to continue life.
* **Excretion** removes waste from the body.
* **Nutrition** means taking in food for energy and growth.
* **Non-living** objects never meet all seven criteria.
* **Dead** organisms met the criteria in the past.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **MRS GREN**: The seven essential life processes of living things.
  * **Respiration**: The cellular process that releases usable energy.
  * **Sensitivity**: The ability to detect and respond to changes.
  * **Non-living**: Objects that have never met life's criteria.
  * **Dead**: Organisms that once lived but no longer do.

Linnaean Classification and Naming
===================================================

* **Classification** sorts living things into seven levels.
* Levels move from **kingdom** down to **species**.
* The seven levels are kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species.
* Each species has a two-part **binomial name**.
* The name uses its **genus** and **species**.
* Binomial names are always written in italics.
* The **genus** begins with a capital letter.
* The **species** is written in lowercase letters.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Classification**: Organising living things into ranked categories.
  * **Kingdom**: The broadest major level in classification.
  * **Binomial Name**: A unique two-part scientific name for an organism.
  * **Genus**: A rank grouping closely related species together.
  * **Species**: The most specific level of classification.

Relatedness and Common Ancestors
===================================================

* More shared levels means closer relatedness.
* Close relatives share a recent **common ancestor**.
* Shared traits and behaviours reveal **relatedness**.
* Internal structures also show how species relate.
* Humans are closely related to bonobos.
* Kangaroos are much more distantly related to humans.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Relatedness**: How closely two species are connected by evolution.
  * **Common Ancestor**: An earlier organism from which species evolved.
  * **Traits**: Physical or behavioural characteristics used for grouping.
