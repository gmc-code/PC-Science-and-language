===================================================
3E The Impact of Invasive Species
===================================================

Ecological Concepts and Definitions
===================================================

* Introduced species are taken outside their natural area.
* Invasive species spread quickly and cause harm.
* Invasive species outcompete native species for resources.
* Resources include food, water, and shelter.
* This can cause local population declines or extinctions.
* Some species are introduced deliberately for farming.
* Others arrive accidentally through cargo or ballast water.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Species**: Closely related organisms capable of interbreeding.
  * **Native Species**: Organisms occurring naturally in a region.
  * **Introduced Species**: Organisms moved beyond their natural range by humans.
  * **Invasive Species**: An introduced species that spreads and harms.
  * **Eradication**: Completely removing a species from an area.

.. cloze::

    1. Organisms occurring naturally are @@native@@ species.
    2. A harmful, fast-spreading species is @@invasive@@.
    3. Completely removing a species is called @@eradication@@.

----

Mechanisms of Introduction and Ecological Impact
===================================================

* Deliberate introductions include farming, pest control, hunting.
* Accidental introductions occur via shipping containers.
* Ballast water also carries species accidentally.
* Generalist predators affect many trophic levels.
* They eat native prey and compete with predators.
* Cane toad tadpoles affect algae in water.
* Adult cane toads eat native insects.
* Their skin toxins poison native predators.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Biological Control**: Releasing a natural enemy to manage pests.
  * **Trophic Level**: An organism's position in a food chain.
  * **Resource Competition**: Organisms struggling for shared, limited resources.

.. cloze::

    1. Species arriving in ballast water are @@accidental@@ introductions.
    2. Invasive species cause @@competition@@ for limited resources.
    3. Using natural enemies on pests is @@biological@@ control.
