3D Changes to Ecosystems and Populations
============================================

The Importance of Plants in Ecosystems
=======================================

* Plants take in carbon dioxide.
* Plants release oxygen into the air.
* Plants feed primary consumers and support biodiversity.
* Trees provide shelter for many organisms.
* Tree roots reduce soil erosion.
* Decomposing trees replenish nutrients in soil.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Biodiversity**: The variety of organisms within an ecosystem.
  * **Erosion**: The removal of topsoil by wind or water.

.. cloze::

    1. Plants absorb carbon dioxide and release @@oxygen@@.
    2. Tree roots prevent topsoil loss through @@erosion@@.
    3. The variety of species is called @@biodiversity@@.

Seasonal Patterns and Population Fluctuations
=================================================

* Seasons change abiotic factors like temperature.
* Water availability also changes with seasons.
* Seasonal changes trigger survival behaviors in organisms.
* Population sizes rise and fall regularly.
* Organisms adapt through migration and dormancy.
* Reproductive timing also helps organisms survive seasons.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Hibernation**: Dormancy triggered by extreme cold and food shortage.
  * **Aestivation**: Dormancy triggered by extreme heat and dryness.
  * **Migration**: Seasonal movement of animals between regions.

.. cloze::

    1. Desert heat causes dormancy called @@aestivation@@.
    2. Humpback whales show @@migration@@ to warmer waters.
    3. Seasonal changes are not sudden @@ecosystem collapse@@.

Ecosystem Regulation by Predators and Pollinators
=================================================

* Pollinators and top predators stabilize food webs.
* Losing pollinators prevents plant reproduction.
* Food shortages then affect all consumers.
* Losing top predators increases prey numbers.
* Too many prey causes overgrazing.
* Overgrazing severely depletes the vegetation.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Pollinator**: An organism transferring pollen between plants.
  * **Predator**: An organism that hunts and regulates prey.
  * **Overgrazing**: Herbivores eating vegetation faster than it recovers.

.. cloze::

    1. Losing @@pollinators@@ prevents plants from reproducing.
    2. Removing predators leads to @@overgrazing@@ by prey.
    3. Pollinators and predators maintain ecological @@balance@@.

Human Impacts: Deforestation and Agriculture
============================================

* Deforestation for farming destroys animal habitats.
* Habitat loss causes declines across trophic levels.
* Monocultures repeatedly drain the same soil nutrients.
* Heavy livestock compact the soil.
* Compacted soil limits roots and water infiltration.
* Compaction increases runoff and soil erosion.
* Fertilizer runoff into water causes eutrophication.
* Algal blooms block sunlight and deplete oxygen.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Deforestation**: The large-scale clearing of forests from land.
  * **Monoculture**: Growing a single crop species repeatedly.
  * **Eutrophication**: Nutrient enrichment of water causing excessive algae.
  * **Algal Bloom**: The rapid growth of algae on water.

.. cloze::

    1. Growing one crop repeatedly is called @@monoculture@@.
    2. Livestock cause soil @@compaction@@, blocking water.
    3. Fertilizer runoff leads to @@eutrophication@@ in water.
