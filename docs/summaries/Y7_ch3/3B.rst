===============================
3B Food Chains and Food Webs
===============================

Understanding Food Chains
=========================

* A food chain is a model that shows a sequence of who-eats-who, representing a single pathway of energy flow in an ecosystem.
* Every food chain begins with a producer (autotroph) that captures light energy from the Sun to make sugar.
* Each sequential step in a food chain represents a trophic level, moving from producers to primary, secondary, and tertiary consumers up to the apex predator.

* **Key Terms:**
  * **Food Chain**: A single sequence showing how energy passes from one organism to another through feeding.
  * **Producer**: An autotroph that converts sunlight into chemical energy/food.
  * **Apex Predator**: An animal at the top of a food chain with no natural predators.
  * **Trophic Level**: The specific step or position an organism occupies in a food sequence.

    2. The arrows in a food chain point in the direction of energy @@flow@@.
    3. An animal at the very end of a food chain that has no natural predators is an @@apex predator@@.

Energy Transfer and Trophic Pyramids
====================================

* As energy moves up through trophic levels, significant energy is used for biological processes or lost as heat and waste.
* Approximately 10% of the energy from one organism is passed on to the consumer in the next trophic level.
* Trophic pyramids visually represent this energy reduction, showing why ecosystems support fewer individuals at higher trophic levels.

* **Key Terms:*

  * **Trophic Pyramid**: A diagram showing the decrease in available energy at each progressive trophic level.
  * **Primary Consumer**: An organism that feeds directly on producers (herbivore).
  * **Secondary Consumer**: An organism that feeds on primary consumers.

.. cloze::

    1. Only about @@10%@@ of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next.
    2. Organisms lose energy through life processes such as growth, movement, and @@waste@@.
    3. Higher trophic levels support a smaller @@population@@ of animals due to energy loss.

Food Webs and Ecosystem Dynamics
================================

* Food webs combine multiple interconnected food chains to model the complex feeding relationships within a biodiverse ecosystem.
* A keystone species plays a critical role in maintaining ecosystem stability; altering its population causes widespread impacts across trophic levels.
* Disruptions to keystone species can trigger a trophic cascade, shifting the balance of predators, prey, and producers.

* **Key Terms:**
  * **Food Web**: A complex network of interconnected food chains showing multiple energy pathways.
  * **Biodiverse**: Containing a wide variety of different living organisms within an ecosystem.
  * **Keystone Species**: A species that has an unusually large effect on the balance and health of its ecosystem.
  * **Trophic Cascade**: Side-effect events that ripple through all trophic levels when a top organism is added or removed.

.. cloze::

    1. A complex diagram showing interconnected feeding pathways is a @@food web@@.
    2. Changing the population of a @@keystone species@@ can dramatically alter the entire ecosystem.
    3. The side-effects that ripple through all trophic levels when top predators change is called a @@trophic cascade@@.
