The Importance of Plants in Ecosystems
=======================================

* Plants take in carbon dioxide, produce oxygen, and support biodiversity by serving as the foundational food source for primary consumers.
* Trees provide added structural stability to terrestrial environments by offering shelter, reducing soil erosion via root networks, and replenishing soil nutrients through decomposition.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Biodiversity**: The variety of living organisms within a given ecosystem.
  * **Erosion**: The physical removal and transport of topsoil by natural forces such as wind or water.

.. cloze::

    1. Plants maintain ecosystem balance by absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing @@oxygen@@.
    2. Tree root systems help stabilize soil, preventing the loss of topsoil through @@erosion@@.
    3. The variety of different living species present within an ecosystem is known as @@biodiversity@@.

Seasonal Patterns and Population Fluctuations
=================================================

* Seasonal shifts in abiotic factors (such as temperature and water availability) trigger distinct survival behaviors in organisms, causing regular fluctuations in population sizes.
* Organisms adapt to seasonal variations through migration, reproductive timing, and metabolic dormancy.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Hibernation**: Metabolic dormancy triggered by extreme cold and reduced food availability.
  * **Aestivation**: Metabolic dormancy triggered by extreme heat and arid conditions.
  * **Migration**: The seasonal movement of animal populations from one region to another.

.. cloze::

    1. Extreme heat and lack of water cause desert organisms to enter a period of dormancy called @@aestivation@@.
    2. Humpback whales exhibit @@migration@@ by traveling to warmer northern waters to raise their young.
    3. Population size fluctuations caused by seasonal changes follow regular cycles rather than sudden @@ecosystem collapse@@.

Ecosystem Regulation by Predators and Pollinators
=================================================

* Keystone functional groups, including pollinators and top predators, maintain stability across food webs.
* Removal of pollinators prevents plant reproduction, causing food shortages that ripple upward through primary and secondary consumers.
* Removal of top predators causes prey populations to increase rapidly, resulting in overgrazing and severe vegetation depletion.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Pollinator**: An organism that transfers pollen between plants to enable fertilization and reproduction.
  * **Predator**: An organism that hunts and feeds on other living organisms, regulating prey numbers.
  * **Overgrazing**: Excessive consumption of vegetation by herbivores without sufficient recovery time.

.. cloze::

    1. Disappearance of @@pollinators@@ directly prevents plant reproduction and collapses primary food sources.
    2. The removal of predators causes prey numbers to grow excessively, leading to @@overgrazing@@.
    3. Both pollinators and predators serve critical roles in regulating population sizes and maintaining ecological @@balance@@.

Human Impacts: Deforestation and Agriculture
============================================

* Habitat destruction, such as deforestation for agriculture, causes rapid and long-term population declines across multiple trophic levels.
* Agricultural monocultures reduce soil quality by repeatedly depleting identical nutrient profiles without proper replenishment cycles.
* Heavy livestock compacts soil, limiting root expansion and restricting water infiltration, which increases surface runoff and soil erosion.
* Excess agricultural fertilizers washed into bodies of water cause eutrophication, triggering algal blooms that block sunlight and deplete dissolved oxygen.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Deforestation**: The large-scale removal or clearing of forests from a land area.
  * **Monoculture**: The agricultural practice of growing a single crop species repeatedly over a given area.
  * **Eutrophication**: Nutrient enrichment of aquatic environments leading to excessive microbial and algal growth.
  * **Algal Bloom**: The rapid proliferation of algae on the surface of a body of water.

.. cloze::

    1. Growing a single crop continuously on the same land is an agricultural practice called @@monoculture@@.
    2. Hard-hoofed livestock cause soil @@compaction@@, preventing water from seeping into the ground.
    3. Runoff containing agricultural fertilizers leads to @@eutrophication@@, which starves aquatic ecosystems of oxygen.
