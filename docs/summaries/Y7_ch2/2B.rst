======================================
2B How Biologists Classify Organisms
======================================

The Criteria for Life
==================================

* **Living** things must satisfy all seven life processes known as **MRS GREN**.
* **Movement** means moving parts, **respiration** releases energy, and **sensitivity** detects changes.
* **Growth** increases size, **reproduction** creates offspring, **excretion** removes waste, and **nutrients** come from food.
* **Non-living** objects never meet all seven criteria, whereas **dead** organisms met them in the past.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **MRS GREN**: The seven essential life processes of living things.
  * **Respiration**: The cellular process releasing usable energy.
  * **Sensitivity**: The ability to detect and respond to changes.
  * **Non-living**: Objects that have never met the criteria for life.
  * **Dead**: Organisms that once met all life processes but do not anymore.

Linnaean Classification and Naming
===================================================

* **Classification** organizes living things into seven levels, moving from a broad **kingdom** down to a specific **species**.
* Every organism receives a two-part **binomial name** using its **genus** and **species** written in italics.
* The **genus** is capitalized first, and the **species** follows in lowercase to form the unique scientific name.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Classification**: Organizing living things into hierarchical categories.
  * **Kingdom**: The broadest major level in biological classification.
  * **Binomial Name**: A unique two-part scientific name for an organism.
  * **Genus**: A rank grouping closely related species together.
  * **Species**: The specific classification level for an individual organism.


.. cloze::

   1. Scientific names use a @@binomial name@@ format containing the @@genus@@ and species.
   2. Broad ranks start at the @@kingdom@@ level and narrow down to a single @@species@@.
   3. The @@genus@@ is capitalized first, while the @@species@@ is written in lowercase.
   4. Biological @@classification@@ groups organisms based on shared traits across each @@kingdom@@.

Relatedness and Common Ancestors
===================================================

* Organisms sharing more classification levels are more closely related and share a recent **common ancestor**.
* Shared physical traits, internal structures, and behaviors reveal evolutionary **relatedness** between different animals.
* For example, humans share a closer **common ancestor** with bonobos than with kangaroos.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Relatedness**: How closely connected two species are by evolution.
  * **Common Ancestor**: An earlier organism from which distinct species evolved.
  * **Traits**: Physical or behavioral characteristics used to group organisms.

   2. Humans share a recent @@common ancestor@@ with bonobos rather than @@kangaroos@@.
   3. Closely related organisms share a recent @@common ancestor@@ that shows their genetic @@relatedness@@.
