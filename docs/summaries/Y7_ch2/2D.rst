2D Discovering the Kingdoms
==============================

History of Classification and Microscopy
====================================================================

* Biological classification has continuously evolved throughout history as new scientific tools and observations emerged.
* Early taxonomy began with philosophers like Aristotle and expanded with naturalists like Linnaeus, but was initially limited to visible organisms.
* The invention and advancement of microscopes in the 1600s allowed scientists to observe microscopic life and cellular structures.
* Learning how microscopy contributed to taxonomy helps us understand the true complexity of living organisms and their evolutionary relationships.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Microscope**: An instrument used to view very small objects that cannot be seen with the naked eye.
  * **Microorganisms**: Organisms that require a microscope to see, also known as microbes.

The Animal Kingdom
==================================

* Animals obtain nutrition by consuming other organisms and can be broadly categorized as invertebrates or vertebrates.
* Vertebrates belong to the phylum Chordata and are further divided into five distinct classes: mammals, reptiles, fish, amphibians, and birds.
* Unique traits define each class, though some animals like the egg-laying platypus possess combinations of features that make them tricky to categorize.
* Observing microscopic genetic material allows scientists to identify deeper cellular similarities that visible physical traits might obscure.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Phylum**: A rank in biological classification situated directly below kingdom.
  * **Invertebrates**: Animals that lack a backbone.
  * **Vertebrates**: Animals that possess a backbone.


.. cloze::

   1. All vertebrates belong to the phylum @@Chordata@@.
   2. Animals without a backbone are classified as @@invertebrates@@.
   3. Observing microscopic @@genetic@@ material helps classify organisms with unusual traits.

The Plant Kingdom
==================================

* Plants make up a diverse kingdom of organisms that generate their own nutrition by converting sunlight into energy.
* Angiosperms are flowering plants that produce seeds enclosed within protective fruit, whereas gymnosperms produce seeds on structures like cones.
* Non-flowering plants like ferns and mosses reproduce using tiny units called spores.
* The development of microscopes enabled scientists to discover and detailedly study these microscopic spore-forming structures.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Angiosperms**: Plants that produce flowers and seeds surrounded by a protective fruit.
  * **Gymnosperms**: Non-flowering plants that produce seeds on structures such as cones.
  * **Spores**: Tiny reproductive units capable of developing into a new individual independently.
  * **Pollination**: The transfer of pollen between plants to enable fertilization.


.. cloze::

   1. Plants generate their own nutrients using energy from @@sunlight@@.
   2. Angiosperms produce seeds surrounded by fruit, while @@gymnosperms@@ produce seeds inside cones.
   3. Microscopes helped scientists discover and study plants that reproduce using @@spores@@.

The Protist Kingdom
==================================

* As microscopes improved, scientists discovered organisms displaying a puzzling mix of both plant and animal features.
* The kingdom Protista was created as a third kingdom to house these organisms that did not fit neatly elsewhere.
* Protists are highly diverse and include plant-like algae, animal-like protozoa such as amoebas, and fungus-like slime moulds.
* Organisms like *Euglena* can create energy from sunlight like a plant, yet move and eat like an animal.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Protista**: A kingdom containing organisms that do not fit into the animal, plant, or fungi kingdoms.
  * **Protozoa**: Animal-like protists, such as amoebas, that can alter their shape and move to capture food.


.. cloze::

   1. The kingdom Protista was created because some microscopic species blurred the line between @@plants@@ and animals.
   2. Algae produce food using sunlight, while @@protozoa@@ can move and ingest nutrients.
   3. Organisms like *Euglena* are classified as @@protists@@ because they possess features of multiple kingdoms.

The Fungi Kingdom
==================================

* Fungi were originally classified as plants because they grow out of the ground.
* Scientists later redefined the plant kingdom after discovering that fungi do not use sunlight for energy, but absorb nutrients from decaying matter.
* The fungi kingdom includes multicellular filamentous structures like mushrooms and moulds, as well as single-celled yeasts.
* Precise genus-level identification is critical for fungi because edible mushrooms can closely resemble deadly poisonous species.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Fungi**: A kingdom of organisms that gain nutrition by absorbing organic matter rather than through photosynthesis.
  * **Yeasts**: Single-celled microscopic fungi found on plants, in soil, and used in food production.


.. cloze::

   1. Fungi do not make food from sunlight, but instead absorb energy from @@decaying@@ matter.
   2. Moulds and mushrooms grow as filaments, whereas @@yeasts@@ are single-celled fungi.
   3. Detailed identification down to the @@genus@@ level is necessary because some mushrooms are toxic.

The Monera Kingdom and Three Domains
==================================

* High-powered microscopes revealed that bacteria lack a cell nucleus, leading to the creation of the kingdom Monera.
* Modern DNA analysis demonstrated that the Monera group actually consists of two vastly different evolutionary branches: Bacteria and Archaea.
* True bacteria inhabit everyday environments like soil and water, whereas ancient archaea thrive in extreme environments like hot springs.
* Modern biological taxonomy now organizes life into three top-level domains: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.

* **Key Terms:**

  * **Monera**: A historical kingdom comprising microscopic organisms that lack a cell nucleus.
  * **Bacteria**: Single-celled microorganisms found in widespread everyday environments.
  * **Archaea**: Microscopic single-celled organisms often living in extreme conditions like acid lakes.
