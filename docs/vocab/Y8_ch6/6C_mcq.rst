    .. multichoice::

        What are energy by-products?

        [x] Secondary forms of energy produced during energy transfers or transformations. | Correct! By-products are secondary energy forms generated during energy processes.
        [ ] The primary energy source fed into a device. | Incorrect. The primary energy going into a system is input energy.
        [ ] Energy that is permanently destroyed during an operation. | Incorrect. Energy cannot be destroyed according to the law of conservation of energy.
        [ ] Materials used to build energy-generating power plants. | Incorrect. By-products refer to energy forms, not physical construction materials.

    .. multichoice::

        Output energy that is specifically needed and intended for a clear purpose is called:

        [ ] Waste energy | Incorrect. Waste energy is output energy that is not useful to us.
        [x] Useful energy | Correct! Useful energy is the output energy required to perform a specific function.
        [ ] Thermal conduction | Incorrect. Conduction is a method of heat transfer, not a category of output energy.
        [ ] Input energy | Incorrect. Input energy is the energy entering the system, not coming out.

    .. multichoice::

        Waste energy is best described as output energy that:

        [ ] Is absorbed and converted into chemical fuel. | Incorrect. Waste energy is dissipated into surroundings, not stored as fuel.
        [ ] Equals more than the total input energy provided. | Incorrect. Output energy cannot exceed input energy.
        [x] Is not useful to us, usually released as heat, sound, or light. | Correct! Waste energy is unintended output energy released to the environment.
        [ ] Is directly harnessed to power secondary devices. | Incorrect. Useful energy is harnessed; waste energy is unwanted loss.

    .. multichoice::

        What does it mean for a device or system to be energy efficient?

        [x] Maximising useful output energy while creating the smallest possible amount of waste energy. | Correct! Energy efficiency minimizes wasted energy while maximizing useful work.
        [ ] Converting 100% of input energy into sound and light by-products. | Incorrect. Producing mostly by-products like sound and light usually represents low efficiency.
        [ ] Using the largest amount of input energy possible regardless of output. | Incorrect. Efficiency is about maximizing output relative to input, not consuming maximum power.
        [ ] Creating new energy to replace lost output. | Incorrect. Energy cannot be created.

    .. multichoice::

        Which process transfers thermal energy through direct contact between particles?

        [ ] Convection | Incorrect. Convection occurs through the bulk movement of fluids (liquids or gases).
        [x] Conduction | Correct! Conduction transfers heat via direct collision and contact between neighboring particles.
        [ ] Radiation | Incorrect. Radiation transfers heat using electromagnetic waves without needing particles.
        [ ] Transformation | Incorrect. Transformation refers broadly to energy changing forms.

    .. multichoice::

        How is heat transferred during convection?

        [ ] Through electromagnetic waves traveling across empty space | Incorrect. Radiation transfers heat through electromagnetic waves.
        [x] Through the movement of liquids or gases. | Correct! Convection relies on the physical movement and circulation of fluid particles.
        [ ] By storing chemical energy in atomic bonds | Incorrect. Chemical energy storage does not define heat transfer.
        [ ] Through direct contact of particles in a stationary solid | Incorrect. Heat transfer through direct particle contact in solids is conduction.

    .. multichoice::

        How does radiation transfer heat differently from conduction and convection?

        [ ] Radiation requires dense solids to transfer thermal energy. | Incorrect. Conduction works best in solids, whereas radiation needs no medium.
        [x] Radiation transfers heat via electromagnetic waves without requiring direct contact between particles. | Correct! Radiation uses electromagnetic waves and can travel through a vacuum.
        [ ] Radiation only occurs when liquids boil into gases. | Incorrect. Radiation does not require phase changes or liquids.
        [ ] Radiation converts heat energy directly into gravitational potential energy. | Incorrect. Radiation is a method of heat transfer, not a gravitational conversion.

    .. multichoice::

        In a standard incandescent light bulb, light is the intended useful energy. What is the primary waste energy by-product?

        [ ] Sound energy | Incorrect. Incandescent bulbs produce negligible sound energy.
        [x] Thermal energy (heat) | Correct! Most energy in an incandescent bulb is wasted as heat rather than converted to light.
        [ ] Elastic energy | Incorrect. Bulbs do not store or release elastic energy.
        [ ] Nuclear energy | Incorrect. Light bulbs operate on electrical energy, not nuclear reactions.

    .. multichoice::

        If a machine receives 100 J of input energy and releases 80 J of heat (waste) and 20 J of motion (useful), what is the total output energy?

        [ ] 80 J | Incorrect. 80 J is only the waste energy portion.
        [ ] 20 J | Incorrect. 20 J is only the useful energy portion.
        [x] 100 J | Correct! Total output energy equals useful energy plus waste energy (20 J + 80 J = 100 J).
        [ ] 120 J | Incorrect. Total output energy cannot exceed total input energy.

    .. multichoice::

        Which energy form is commonly an unwanted by-product in electrical appliances like computers and televisions?

        [ ] Chemical potential energy | Incorrect. Appliances do not produce chemical potential energy as a waste by-product.
        [ ] Gravitational potential energy | Incorrect. Running appliances does not generate gravitational potential energy.
        [x] Thermal energy | Correct! Excess heat is a common wasted energy by-product in electronic appliances.
        [ ] Elastic potential energy | Incorrect. Electronics do not release elastic energy during normal operation.
