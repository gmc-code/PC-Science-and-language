========================================
Minor Clauses
========================================

| A sentence is like a complete action story. It needs two things:

- A finite verb - A "time-stamped" action word that tells you when something happens (is, was, melts, reacted).
- A Mood structure - A combination of a who/what (Subject) and that time-stamped verb that lets you make a statement, ask a question, or give a command ("The ice is melting." or "Is the ice melting?").

| A minor clause leaves out those two things. It doesn't tell a full time-stamped story.
| Instead of a full story, it just acts as a label, a quick warning, or a heading:

| Full story (Major clause): "The acid is hot." (Has a time-stamped verb "is" and tells a full story).
| Minor clause: "Hot acid!" (No time-stamped verb, no full story—just a quick warning label).

Single verbs as a major clause
--------------------------------

| Single-word commands like "Run!" or "Jump!" are actually major clauses, not minor clauses.
| Even though they are just one word, they carry a full Mood structure and a Finite verb.
| In imperative clauses (commands), the Subject is the person being spoken to: (You).
| "Run!" really means "(You) run!"
| "Jump!" really means "(You) jump!"
| Because the Subject "You" is understood by both the speaker and the listener, the clause has a complete Mood element: Subject (You) + Finite Process (Run).
