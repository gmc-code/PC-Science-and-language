Biology: Classification of Arachnids
======================================================

Arachnids are a major class of joint-legged invertebrate animals belonging to the phylum Arthropoda. Unlike insects, all arachnids possess eight jointed legs, two main body sections (the cephalothorax and abdomen), and no wings or antennae. Scientists classify the class Arachnida into several distinct orders based on their body structures, venom mechanisms, and silk-producing abilities. The three most common subclasses are spiders, scorpions, and ticks or mites.

**Subclass Araneae: Spiders**

The most widespread group of arachnids is **spiders**, which are easily identified by their narrow waist connecting the cephalothorax and abdomen. Spiders possess specialized organs called spinnerets at the back of their body, which allow them to produce strong silk thread for weaving webs, capturing prey, and building egg sacs. Nearly all spiders have fangs capable of injecting venom to immobilize insects, though only a tiny fraction are dangerous to humans.

**Subclass Scorpiones: Scorpions**

A second distinct group of arachnids is **scorpions**, which are adapted for predatory hunting in warm and dry environments. Scorpions are recognized by their large, claw-like pedipalps used for grasping prey and a long, segmented tail that curves over their back. This tail ends in a venomous stinger used for defense and hunting. Unlike spiders, scorpions do not spin silk, and females carry their live-born young on their backs for protection.

**Subclass Acari: Ticks and Mites**

The final major group consists of **ticks and mites**, which are tiny arachnids with fused body segments that form a single oval shape. Ticks are larger parasitic arachnids that attach to animals and humans to feed on blood, often transmitting diseases in the process. Mites are microscopic organisms found in soil, water, and house dust, serving either as scavengers that break down organic matter or as microscopic parasites.

Chemistry: Classification of Matter
======================================================

All physical objects in the universe are made of matter, which is defined as anything that has mass and takes up space. Scientists classify all matter into two primary categories based on chemical composition: pure substances and mixtures. Each of these main classes is further divided into specific subclasses based on how their constituent particles are combined and arranged.

**Class Pure Substances: Elements and Compounds**

A **pure substance** consists of only one type of particle and has a constant, fixed composition throughout. Pure substances are divided into two subclasses: elements and compounds. **Elements** are the simplest pure substances, made of only one type of atom (such as gold, oxygen, and carbon) that cannot be broken down by chemical means. **Compounds** consist of two or more different elements chemically bonded together in fixed ratios (such as water or carbon dioxide), which can only be separated through chemical reactions.

**Class Mixtures: Homogeneous and Heterogeneous**

A **mixture** consists of two or more physical substances combined together without chemical bonding, meaning they can be separated by physical methods like filtration or evaporation. Mixtures are divided into two subclasses: homogeneous and heterogeneous. **Homogeneous mixtures** (such as saltwater or air) have a uniform appearance throughout because their particles are evenly distributed. **Heterogeneous mixtures** (such as muddy water or salad) have visible boundaries and non-uniform distributions of their component parts.

Physics: Classification of Electromagnetic Radiation
======================================================

Electromagnetic radiation consists of waves of magnetic and electric energy traveling through space at the speed of light. Physicists classify all forms of electromagnetic energy into a continuous hierarchy called the electromagnetic spectrum. This spectrum is categorized into distinct wave classes based on wavelength, frequency, and energy level, ranging from low-energy radio waves to high-energy gamma rays.

**Low-Energy Wave Classes: Radio, Microwaves, and Infrared**

The lower section of the spectrum consists of **low-energy waves** characterized by long wavelengths and low frequencies. **Radio waves** have the longest wavelengths and are used primarily for broadcasting signals and telecommunications. **Microwaves** have shorter wavelengths used for heating food and radar technology, while **infrared radiation** is emitted as heat by warm objects and is felt as thermal energy.

**Visible Light Class: The Visible Spectrum**

The central band of the spectrum is **visible light**, which is the narrow range of electromagnetic frequencies that human eyes can detect. Visible light is further sub-classified into colors—ranging from red (lowest frequency) to violet (highest frequency)—which combine to form white light. This class of radiation is essential for photosynthesis in plants and human vision.

**High-Energy Wave Classes: Ultraviolet, X-rays, and Gamma Rays**

The upper end of the spectrum consists of **high-energy waves** characterized by short wavelengths and high frequencies. **Ultraviolet radiation** is produced by the sun and can cause sunburns or trigger vitamin D production. **X-rays** possess higher energy that allows them to pass through soft human tissue to image bones, while **gamma rays** are the most powerful form of radiation, released by nuclear reactions and dying stars.


Earth Science: Classification of Rocks
======================================================

The Earth's crust is composed of solid geological materials called rocks. Geologists classify all rocks into three primary classes based on their specific origin and formation pathway within the rock cycle: igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic. Each major class is further divided into subclasses determined by the physical environment and conditions under which the rock formed.

**Class Igneous Rocks: Intrusive and Extrusive**

**Igneous rocks** form when molten rock cools and solidifies into crystal structures. This class is divided into two subclasses: intrusive and extrusive. **Intrusive igneous rocks** (like granite) form slowly deep underground from magma, creating large, visible mineral crystals. **Extrusive igneous rocks** (like basalt or obsidian) form rapidly at the Earth's surface from cooling lava, resulting in tiny crystals or a smooth, glassy texture.

**Class Sedimentary Rocks: Clastic, Chemical, and Organic**

**Sedimentary rocks** form at the Earth's surface through the accumulation and compaction of mineral fragments and debris. This class is divided into three subclasses: clastic, chemical, and organic. **Clastic rocks** (like sandstone) form from compressed rock fragments, **chemical rocks** (like rock salt) form when dissolved minerals precipitate out of evaporating water, and **organic rocks** (like coal) form from compressed plant and animal remains.

**Class Metamorphic Rocks: Foliated and Non-Foliated**

**Metamorphic rocks** form when pre-existing rocks are transformed by intense heat and extreme pressure deep inside the Earth without actually melting. This class is divided into two subclasses based on texture: foliated and non-foliated. **Foliated rocks** (like slate and schist) feature visible parallel layers or bands created by aligned minerals, while **non-foliated rocks** (like marble) have a uniform, non-layered crystal structure.
