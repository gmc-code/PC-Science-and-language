        What do Energy Star rating labels show consumers when comparing appliances?

        [x] How energy efficient an appliance is compared to other models of similar size. | Correct! Star ratings allow easy comparison of efficiency between similar devices.
        [ ] The exact retail price of the appliance in store. | Incorrect. Star ratings reflect energy performance, not retail cost.
        [ ] The maximum weight capacity of the appliance. | Incorrect. Capacity is measured in physical units, not star ratings.
        [ ] The number of years the appliance will last before breaking. | Incorrect. Star ratings measure efficiency, not product lifespan.

    .. multichoice::

        Which unit of measurement is used on energy labels to show total yearly electricity consumption?

        [ ] Watts (W) | Incorrect. Watts measure power (the rate of energy use), not total consumption over time.
        [x] Kilowatt-hours (kWh) | Correct! Yearly energy consumption is measured in kilowatt-hours.
        [ ] Volts (V) | Incorrect. Volts measure electrical potential difference.
        [ ] Joules per second (J/s) | Incorrect. Joules per second is equivalent to watts, which measures power rate.

    .. multichoice::

        In physics and household electricity, power is defined as:

        [ ] The total money spent on electricity each month. | Incorrect. That is the electricity bill or running cost.
        [x] The rate at which energy is transformed by a device, measured in watts. | Correct! Power is the rate of energy transformation over time.
        [ ] The maximum amount of heat trapped by wall insulation. | Incorrect. Insulation reduces heat flow, but is not power.
        [ ] The total amount of solar radiation absorbed by concrete floors. | Incorrect. This relates to thermal mass and passive heating.

    .. multichoice::

        How can the running cost of an electrical appliance be calculated?

        [ ] By dividing the power rating by the star rating. | Incorrect. Star ratings are comparative visual guides, not numerical multipliers.
        [x] By multiplying energy used by the electricity unit cost. | Correct! Running cost = Total energy consumed × Cost per energy unit.
        [ ] By subtracting the wattage from the total energy bill. | Incorrect. You must multiply energy consumption by the electricity tariff rate.
        [ ] By multiplying thermal mass by total operating hours. | Incorrect. Thermal mass is a building property, not an appliance power calculation.

    .. multichoice::

        What is passive solar building design?

        [ ] Installing large solar panels to power high-wattage air conditioners. | Incorrect. Passive design works naturally without relying on electrical appliances.
        [x] Using natural sunlight and air movement to warm and cool homes without electrical power. | Correct! Passive solar design utilizes climate and building layout to control temperature.
        [ ] Using electric radiators powered directly by a solar battery system. | Incorrect. Active solar uses powered hardware; passive design relies on structural features.
        [ ] Sealing a house completely without any windows or air vents. | Incorrect. Passive design uses natural ventilation for cooling.

    .. multichoice::

        Which of the following materials is an example of high thermal mass used in building design?

        [ ] Fiberglass wool | Incorrect. Fiberglass wool is an insulating material, not a high thermal mass material.
        [x] Concrete or brick | Correct! Dense materials like concrete, brick, and mudbrick absorb heat and release it slowly.
        [ ] Aluminium foil | Incorrect. Foil acts as a radiant reflective barrier rather than storing thermal mass.
        [ ] Polystyrene foam | Incorrect. Foam is a lightweight insulator with low thermal storage capacity.

    .. multichoice::

        What is the primary role of insulation in walls and ceilings?

        [ ] Generating thermal energy inside the room during winter. | Incorrect. Insulation does not generate heat; it slows heat transfer.
        [x] Trapping air to prevent unwanted heat loss or gain. | Correct! Insulation slows down thermal conduction and convection into or out of a home.
        [ ] Increasing the electrical power rate of household devices. | Incorrect. Insulation affects thermal transfer, not electrical appliance wattage.
        [ ] Reflecting sunlight into the house to increase light energy. | Incorrect. Light reflection is not the function of standard wall and roof insulation.

    .. multichoice::

        What is the purpose of conducting a household energy audit?

        [ ] To calculate the water pressure in kitchen and bathroom pipes. | Incorrect. Energy audits evaluate energy efficiency, not water pressure.
        [x] To inspect energy usage in a home and identify where energy waste can be reduced. | Correct! An energy audit locates drafty areas, inefficient devices, and thermal losses.
        [ ] To measure the total weight of all home appliances. | Incorrect. Appliance weight is irrelevant to energy auditing.
        [ ] To replace all electrical wiring with higher-wattage cables. | Incorrect. Audits identify behavioral and structural changes to save energy.

    .. multichoice::

        If Appliance A has a 5-star rating and Appliance B has a 2-star rating (both same size and type), what does this indicate?

        [x] Appliance A uses less energy to perform the same function than Appliance B. | Correct! More stars indicate higher efficiency and lower running costs.
        [ ] Appliance B costs less money to run each year than Appliance A. | Incorrect. Lower star ratings generally mean higher running costs.
        [ ] Appliance A operates at a higher wattage and wastes more energy. | Incorrect. Higher star ratings mean less wasted energy.
        [ ] Both appliances consume the exact same amount of electricity. | Incorrect. Star ratings distinguish energy performance between models.

    .. multichoice::

        How do thermal mass materials help maintain comfortable room temperatures across day and night?

        [ ] They generate electricity when exposed to direct sunlight. | Incorrect. Photovoltaic cells generate electricity, not thermal mass.
        [x] They absorb heat slowly during the day and release it slowly as temperatures drop at night. | Correct! High thermal mass moderates internal temperature fluctuations naturally.
        [ ] They reflect 100% of heat energy away from the building completely. | Incorrect. Thermal mass absorbs heat rather than reflecting it away.
        [ ] They convert sound energy into kinetic energy to circulate air. | Incorrect. Thermal mass deals exclusively with heat absorption and re-radiation.
