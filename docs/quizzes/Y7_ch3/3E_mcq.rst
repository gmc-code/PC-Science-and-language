    .. multichoice::

        What is a native species?

        [x] An organism that occurs naturally in a specific region. | Correct! Native species belong naturally to their region.
        [ ] An organism transported outside its natural area by humans. | Incorrect. That describes an introduced species.
        [ ] An organism that has been completely removed from an area. | Incorrect. Removal describes eradication.
        [ ] A natural enemy released to control pests. | Incorrect. That describes biological control.

    .. multichoice::

        What is an introduced species?

        [ ] An organism that occurs naturally in a region. | Incorrect. That describes a native species.
        [ ] An organism that no longer exists. | Incorrect. That describes an extinct species.
        [x] An organism transported beyond its natural geographical area by human activity. | Correct! Introduced species are brought in by people.
        [ ] An organism at the top of a food chain. | Incorrect. That describes an apex predator.

    .. multichoice::

        What makes an introduced species invasive?

        [ ] It is smaller than native species. | Incorrect. Size does not make a species invasive.
        [x] It spreads rapidly and causes harm to native species or ecosystems. | Correct! Invasive species cause environmental or economic harm.
        [ ] It only lives in water. | Incorrect. Invasive species can live on land or in water.
        [ ] It was introduced accidentally. | Incorrect. Both deliberate and accidental introductions can become invasive.

    .. multichoice::

        What is eradication?

        [ ] The spread of a species into a new area. | Incorrect. Eradication means removing a species.
        [ ] Competition for limited resources. | Incorrect. That describes resource competition.
        [ ] The movement of seeds by wind. | Incorrect. Eradication is not related to seed movement.
        [x] The complete removal of a species from an area. | Correct! Eradication removes a target species completely.

    .. multichoice::

        Which of these is an example of an accidental introduction?

        [x] Organisms carried in cargo shipping or ballast water. | Correct! These organisms are transported unintentionally.
        [ ] Releasing an animal for pest control. | Incorrect. That is a deliberate introduction.
        [ ] Bringing animals in for farming. | Incorrect. That is a deliberate introduction.
        [ ] Releasing animals for hunting. | Incorrect. That is a deliberate introduction.

    .. multichoice::

        What is biological control?

        [ ] Using chemical sprays to kill weeds. | Incorrect. Biological control uses living organisms or pathogens.
        [ ] Moving native species to a new area. | Incorrect. Biological control targets pests.
        [x] Releasing a natural enemy or pathogen to manage a pest population. | Correct! The cane toad was introduced for this reason.
        [ ] Planting a single crop repeatedly. | Incorrect. That describes monoculture.

    .. multichoice::

        How do invasive species cause native populations to decline?

        [ ] By adding extra food to the ecosystem. | Incorrect. Invasive species reduce available resources.
        [ ] By helping native species reproduce. | Incorrect. Invasive species harm native species.
        [ ] By increasing the amount of shelter available. | Incorrect. They compete for shelter.
        [x] By outcompeting them for food, water, and shelter. | Correct! Resource competition can lead to local extinctions.

    .. multichoice::

        What is resource competition?

        [x] The struggle between organisms for shared, limited resources such as food and shelter. | Correct! Invasive species often outcompete native species.
        [ ] The complete removal of a species. | Incorrect. That describes eradication.
        [ ] The release of a pest's natural enemy. | Incorrect. That describes biological control.
        [ ] A species occurring naturally in an area. | Incorrect. That describes a native species.

    .. multichoice::

        How do cane toads affect multiple trophic levels?

        [ ] They only eat plants as adults. | Incorrect. Adult cane toads eat native insects.
        [x] Tadpoles affect algae, adults eat native insects, and their skin toxins poison native predators. | Correct! Cane toads disrupt many levels at once.
        [ ] They act as decomposers in the soil. | Incorrect. Cane toads are consumers, not decomposers.
        [ ] They only compete with other toads. | Incorrect. Their impact reaches across the food web.

    .. multichoice::

        How do broad generalist predators alter food webs?

        [ ] By eating only one type of prey. | Incorrect. Generalist predators eat many types of prey.
        [ ] By producing their own food. | Incorrect. Predators are consumers.
        [x] By eating native prey and competing directly with native predators. | Correct! They affect many trophic levels at once.
        [ ] By increasing native prey populations. | Incorrect. They reduce native prey numbers.
